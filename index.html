Why TIG Gloves Matter

TIG welding demands fine motor control. Unlike MIG or stick welding, you're feeding filler rod with one hand while controlling the torch with the other. Your gloves need to protect you from heat and sparks without robbing you of the finger feel that makes a clean weld possible.

A good pair of TIG gloves shields your hands from radiant heat and spatter, lets you feel the filler rod and torch controls, stays comfortable through long sessions, and lasts through daily use without falling apart.

Key Features to Look For

1. Cuff Length

The cuff is your first line of defense against sparks and radiant heat. Look for an extended cuff of at least 10–13 cm beyond a standard glove length. A total glove length around 35 cm covers your wrist and forearm, where spatter loves to land. Short cuffs leave your wrist exposed — the most common burn spot for TIG welders.

2. Leather Quality

TIG gloves are made from premium leather for a reason: it resists heat, stays flexible, and provides the thin profile you need for dexterity. The leather should feel supple out of the box, not stiff or cardboard-like. Avoid gloves where the palm feels thick and bulky — you'll lose the filler-rod feel that TIG requires.

3. Stitching

Heat destroys ordinary thread. Look for gloves stitched with DuPont™ Kevlar® or similar heat-resistant thread. Kevlar stitching won't melt or unravel when exposed to sparks and radiant heat, which dramatically extends glove life. Check the seams: they should be tight, even, and reinforced at stress points (thumb crotch, fingertips).

4. Finger Design

Two details separate great TIG gloves from average ones:

  • Seamless index finger — eliminates the bulky seam that gets in the way of precise filler-rod feeding
  • Winged thumb — gives your thumb a natural range of motion for torch manipulation

These small design choices add up to noticeably better control.

5. Fit

TIG gloves should fit snugly but not tight. Too loose and you lose feel; too tight and your hands fatigue. See the sizing section below.

How to Choose Size: L vs XL

Most TIG welding gloves come in L and XL. Here's how to pick:

  1. Measure your dominant hand across the knuckles (excluding the thumb), using a flexible tape measure or a strip of paper.
  2. Compare to the size chart below.
  3. When in doubt, size down. A slightly snug TIG glove breaks in within a few hours of use. A loose glove never gets better.
  4. Consider what you weld. Overhead work favors a snugger fit so the glove doesn't slide. For bench work, either size works.

Care Tips

  • Let them dry naturally. Never put leather welding gloves on a heater or in direct sun — heat cracks the leather.
  • Brush off spatter after each session with a stiff brush.
  • Store flat, not crumpled, to keep the shape.
  • Rotate pairs if you weld daily — leather lasts longer when it rests between uses.
  • Replace when: the palm thins out, stitching unravels, or you feel heat through the leather. Worn gloves are a safety hazard, not a badge of honor.

FAQ

Can I use TIG gloves for MIG welding?

Yes — if the cuff is long enough and the leather is heat-resistant. Many welders use one quality pair for TIG, MIG, and MAG work.

How long should TIG gloves last?

With daily professional use, expect 2–4 months from a good pair. Kevlar stitching and quality leather extend life significantly. Hobbyists may get a year or more.

Are gauntlet-style (long cuff) gloves always better?

For most welding, yes. The extra cuff protects your forearm from spatter and UV. Short-cuff "driver" style gloves are fine for light TIG tack work but leave you exposed during longer beads.

My gloves feel stiff — is that normal?

New premium leather gloves break in within a few hours of use. Flex them by hand before your first session to speed things up. If they're still cardboard-stiff after a day of welding, the leather quality is the problem.

Should I wear a liner underneath?

For most TIG work, no — liners reduce the finger feel you need. If you're doing extended high-amperage work, a thin FR liner adds protection without much bulk.
